```html
<p>Factor scores add another layer of inference. Even if the population parameters were known, the factors are unobserved because specific variation remains. Among linear predictors $\widehat{\mathbf{f}}=\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{x}-\boldsymbol\mu)$ with positive definite $\boldsymbol\Sigma$, expected squared prediction error is minimized at $\mathbf{A}_*=\boldsymbol\Lambda^{\mathsf T}\boldsymbol\Sigma^{-1}$. To see this, use $\operatorname{Cov}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{f})=\boldsymbol\Lambda$ and expand the quadratic expectation. Completing the square gives</p>
$$E\|\mathbf{f}-\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{x}-\boldsymbol\mu)\|^2=\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf{I}_k-\boldsymbol\Lambda^{\mathsf T}\boldsymbol\Sigma^{-1}\boldsymbol\Lambda)+\operatorname{tr}\!\left[(\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{A}_*)\boldsymbol\Sigma(\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{A}_*)^{\mathsf T}\right].$$
<p>Positive definiteness makes the second term nonnegative, uniquely zero at $\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{A}_*$. The prediction-error covariance is $\mathbf{I}_k-\boldsymbol\Lambda^{\mathsf T}\boldsymbol\Sigma^{-1}\boldsymbol\Lambda$. The matrix need not vanish. A calculated score is therefore an estimate of a latent coordinate, not a direct measurement of that coordinate.</p>
```
