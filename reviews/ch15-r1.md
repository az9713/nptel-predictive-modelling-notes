## Findings

### 1. major: 15.1, residual-versus-fitted paragraph

Quoted text: The squared residuals also underestimate their corresponding error variances differently according to leverage

This direction of discrepancy is not valid under heteroscedasticity. Under a correct mean E(e_i^2|X)=(M Omega M^T)_ii, which may exceed or fall below Omega_ii. For an intercept-only two-row fit with Omega=diag(1,100), both residual variances are 25.25, so the first exceeds its error variance.

Required replacement:

<p>Even with a correct mean, squared residuals are not direct observations of the individual error variances. Writing $\mathbf M=\mathbf I-\mathbf H$, we have $E(e_i^2\mid\mathbf X)=(\mathbf M\boldsymbol\Omega\mathbf M^T)_{ii}$. Under homoscedasticity this reduces to $\sigma^2(1-h_{ii})$; under heteroscedasticity the discrepancy from $\sigma_i^2$ can have either sign. Interpret a residual pattern in the context of the fitted mean, leverage, and covariance model.</p>

Status: resolved; verified against the current fragment hash below.

### 2. minor: 15.3, paragraph defining GQ block ranks

Quoted text: Let their sizes be $n_1,n_2$, their common full design rank be $q$

Full rank alone permits n_j=q. The residual variance estimates and reference F require n_j>q in both blocks.

Required replacement:

Append to this paragraph: Assume $n_1>q$ and $n_2>q$ so both residual variance estimates have positive degrees of freedom.
